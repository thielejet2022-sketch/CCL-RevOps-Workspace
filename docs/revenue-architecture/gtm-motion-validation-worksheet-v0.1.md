# CCL GTM Motion Validation Worksheet v0.2

**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Mode:** Candidate / pre-employment
**Design status:** PROPOSED discovery instrument; not approved CCL process.
**Baseline:** [Product × GTM Motion Matrix v0.1](product-gtm-motion-matrix-v0.1.md)

## Purpose and use
Determine which buying journeys require distinct motions and which are variants within a motion. Four motions are candidates, not a required outcome. Complete one motion card per candidate and one case trace per actual buying event. Compare evidence before recommending KEEP, MERGE, SPLIT or DEFER. A motion decision does not automatically require a separate CRM pipeline.

Use a focused working session with commercial, marketing, delivery and finance representatives. Suggested role perspectives are prompts, not verified CCL ownership. Validate them first. Start with one won and one lost/no-decision case per motion, plus a repeat/renewal case where applicable and a routing/capacity exception. This is a qualitative discovery sample, not statistical proof; expand across regions, offerings and time periods when contradictions appear. Synthetic cases may test the worksheet but cannot validate CCL operations.

Record UNKNOWN rather than guessing. Keep confidential case details in an authorized internal location; put sanitized conclusions and permitted source references in this repository.

## 2. Common motion card — complete for each candidate
| ID | Validation question | Capture / inspect |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Who experiences the need, chooses, approves, pays and participates? | Distinct roles; buying organization; participant and payer references; account-link evidence |
| B2 | What triggers purchase and what customer outcome is sought? | Problem, desired impact, critical event; evidence of intent |
| B3 | Is purchase standardized or does it require discovery, design, procurement or negotiation? | Actual steps, stakeholders, seller/design effort; exceptions |
| B4 | What proves readiness for sales/enrollment, and what proves commitment? | Observable entry/exit criteria; accepted order, enrollment or contract policy |

| B12 | Does variation justify a distinct motion or a segment/variant? | Compare buying gates, effort, delivery constraints and economics; state evidence for distinction |

## 3. Four candidate motion tests
All answers currently UNKNOWN unless evidence is explicitly attached.

| Candidate | Most important questions | Evidence requested | Decision to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Direct / assisted enrollment or order | Can the purchaser complete a standardized purchase? What assistance/eligibility checks occur? Does employer payment alter the buying journey? What causes cancellation or failure to attend? | Enrollment/order trail; assistance log; dates; cancellation policy; cohort costs and capacity; payer/participant mapping | Is assisted buying a variant of M1, or materially different? Are orders and enrollment similar enough to group? |
| M2 Organizational program sale | Is there an organizational sponsor and volume commitment? Are procurement, account selling and scheduling materially different from M1? How much design is required? Who owns repeat sponsorship? | Sponsored orders/contracts; sponsor communications; seller hours; volume terms; repeat purchases and organizational links | KEEP M2 only if evidence supports distinct repeatable buying/process/economic requirements; otherwise MERGE with M1 or M3 |
| M3 Consultative solution sale | When does pre-sale design begin? Who approves pursuit effort and delivery feasibility? What scope/impact is agreed? Where do design, implementation and finance handoffs fail? | Won/lost pursuit records; design estimates/actual effort; SOW; resource approvals; handoff packet; delivery/outcome evidence | Which variations belong inside M3, and do any require a distinct motion? |
| M4 Enterprise subscription / licensing sale | What qualifies as contracted recurring revenue? Who deploys the solution? What is included by tier? How are customization, adoption and renewal managed? | Executed terms; entitlement rules; onboarding/usage; support/design effort; eligible renewal cohort; expansion/contraction history | Does subscription selling and post-sale responsibility differ enough to retain M4? Which services are included versus separately sold? |

Suggested perspectives: Marketing for intent/routing; Sales or enrollment service for buying/acceptance; Delivery/product/client success for scope, capacity and impact; Finance for costs, revenue and cash; RevOps for definitions and measurement. Named owners remain to be validated.

## Synthetic-test refinements — v0.2
See [test findings](gtm-motion-synthetic-tests-2026-10-12.md); synthetic tests refine the instrument, not CCL evidence.
- B1/B3: distinguish employer reimbursement or payment from sponsor-led organizational selection/procurement. Record why the buying process warrants a distinct motion.
- B5/case trace: record original motion, final motion, transfer date, accepting owner and eventual outcome. Wrong route and poor fit remain separate.
- B7/B11/economics: reconcile incremental versus allocated cost views; identify whether demand fills existing capacity or creates a new cohort/resource obligation.
- B6: classify each service component as included entitlement, requested exception, approved scope, separately sold, or delivered. Pending requests are not bookings or expansion.
- B9: record deployment readiness, intended learner audience, administrator enablement, learner usage and impact as separate milestones.
- Economic/cash recovery: label OBSERVED or MODELED, specify recognition and cost timing assumptions; missing outflows prevent cash-recovery conclusions.
- Metric readiness: use NOT YET OBSERVABLE for immature outcome or retention windows, not zero.

B10: enrollment and payment reference available in fixture. B11: no enterprise pursuit. B12: employer funding alone does not distinguish a motion.
Recommendation within simulation: MERGE into M1, with employer-funded segment. Do not infer organizational buying from email domain or reimbursement.

## Test B: Passport with entitlement and adoption tension
### Fixture B1
A first-time organization signs a 12-month license for $120,000, plus separately priced $15,000 implementation, on June 1; subscription starts July 1. Invoice June 1 and cash June 15 are stipulated. Two administrators trained July 10; planned first cohort September 1 moves to October 1 because the customer has no available facilitator. Client requests 80 customization hours against 40 included hours. Additional 40 hours are estimated but neither approved, sold nor delivered. Renewal decision has not occurred.
Source B1 is this fixture; all terms/events SYNTHETIC.
B1: L&D sponsor/economic buyer, procurement, two admins, facilitators and learners distinct. B2: scalable internal development need. B3: enterprise procurement, license and deployment plan. B4: named deployment owner and executed agreement; actual facilitator readiness remains deficient. B5: adoption delay caused by customer readiness in this fixture, not proven marketing failure. B6: recurring license, one-time implementation and included customization explicitly separated. B7: facilitator readiness and design capacity. B8: invented seller → implementation → client success; scope exception requires authorized commercial/delivery decision. B9: administrator training is enablement, not learner adoption or impact. B10: contract and deployment references present; actual learner usage UNKNOWN. B11: acquisition and recurring/support economics distinct from projects. B12: KEEP M4 as a candidate in simulation; no approved CCL conclusion.

Economics fixture: acquisition $30,000; annual subscription delivery/support cost $36,000 including the 40 entitled hours; implementation cost $12,000. Subscription contribution $84,000; implementation contribution $3,000; total first-year contribution $87,000 before acquisition, $57,000 after acquisition before shared overhead.
Extra requested hours at an invented loaded cost of $150/hour would add $6,000 if delivered uncompensated, reducing total contribution to $81,000 before acquisition. This is a scenario, not actual delivered cost or guaranteed impact.
Illustrative economic recovery under uniform monthly subscription revenue/cost and implementation contribution at start: ($30,000 - $3,000) / ($84,000 / 12) = 3.86 months after service starts. It is a modeled result, not an observed recovery date. Cash recovery UNKNOWN without outflow schedule; upfront collection alone does not determine cash profitability.
Under the fixture's explicit recurring-line definition, annualized subscription run rate is $120,000; the $15,000 one-time fee is excluded. Do not classify $135,000 as ARR.
GRR/NRR and renewal rate are NOT YET OBSERVABLE: no completed eligible comparison/renewal window. Neither missing adoption nor excess scope proves churn.

Proposed action inside simulation: deployment owner resolves facilitator readiness; commercial/delivery owner reviews included scope and authorizes re-scope, separately paid service or exception. Do not call entitled use an upsell or count unapproved requests as expansion.
Disposition: KEEP M4 candidate; customization is a service/scope exception within the contract, not automatically a new GTM motion. A separately sold custom engagement would be traced as a new buying event without reacquiring the organization.
